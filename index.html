Neuromorphic & Biologically-Inspired AI

What if the best AI architecture already exists, inside your skull? The brain is the best piece of engineering that exists. Biology solved problems billions of years before we wrote our first line of code, and I think applying those principles to today's compute has all the answers. I work on neuro-symbolic approaches, predictive processing, and spiking neural networks because the gap between how brains compute and how GPUs compute is where the interesting stuff lives.

LLM Evaluation & Reliability

How do you trust a system that grades itself? I study the failure modes that emerge when LLMs judge other LLMs, particularly the unintuitive ways retrieval volume distorts evaluation. Turns out, giving a judge more evidence can make it worse, not better.

Quantization & Efficient Representations

There's something deeply satisfying about squeezing big information into small representations without losing what matters. Binary quantization, locality-sensitive hashing, making models run on hardware that has no business running them. How small can you go before things break?

Natural Language Understanding

When a model reads "the trophy doesn't fit in the suitcase because it's too big," does it know what "it" refers to? Language models process text. That's not the same as understanding it. I'm interested in the gap: how commonsense reasoning, sentic computing, and structured knowledge can ground language in meaning beyond statistical pattern matching.
